  1. 01

    It starts on a hillside, not the square

    Coffee grows as bright red cherries on farms in Colombia and Ethiopia, picked by people who can tell a ripe one by feel. Theo buys from a few importers he has met in person, and he will tell you about each of them whether or not you asked.

  2. 02

    Small batches, loud cracks

    Theo roasts in the back, twelve pounds at a time, in a drum roaster older than he is. First crack pops like a snare, and Mags supervises it with the ears of a band director. If the timing is off by a beat, she will let him know.

  3. 03

    Ground to order

    We grind right before we brew, because ground coffee goes stale about as fast as gossip travels around the square. Espresso gets a fine grind, pour-over something closer to sea salt. The grinder is the loudest thing in the shop, until Saturday.

  4. 04

    A steady hand and a timer

    A pour-over takes about four minutes, and we will not rush it. The kettle has a gooseneck for a slow, even stream; the barista brings the patience. It is the only thing in the building anyone does quietly.

  5. 05

    Your seat on the square

    Then it is yours: a table by the front window, the courthouse clock keeping time across the street, and a cup that tastes like somebody cared. Stay as long as you like. Nobody here watches the clock except the courthouse, and it is paid to.
